Traditions & Celebrations

🎭

Mangal Shobhajatra

A vibrant mass procession with giant masks, floats, and colourful banners organised by fine arts students — a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

🎨

Alpona Art

Intricate white rice-paste patterns drawn on floors and courtyards to welcome the new year with purity and artistic beauty.

🛍️

Boishakhi Mela

Traditional fairs bursting with folk music, dance, handicrafts, and local delicacies that bring communities together in open celebration.

🧡

Haal Khata

Traders close old ledgers and open fresh account books, often treating loyal customers to sweets — a symbol of renewal in business.

👘

Red & White Attire

Men wear red-and-white panjabis while women drape red-bordered white sarees, embodying the festival's signature colours.

🎶

Rabindra Sangeet

The morning begins with Tagore's "Esho He Boishakh" echoing through Dhaka and Kolkata, calling everyone to embrace the new year.

Festive Delicacies

🐟

Panta Ilish

Fermented rice with fried hilsa — the iconic Pohela Boishakh breakfast.

🍚

Bhuna Khichuri

Rich, slow-cooked rice and lentils served during monsoon & festive gatherings.

🍘

Pitha

Traditional rice cakes — sweet or savoury — steamed, fried, or griddle-baked.

🍨

Mishti Doi

Sweetened fermented yoghurt in earthen pots, a Bengali classic.

🧆

Fuchka

Crispy hollow puris filled with spiced tamarind water — street-food royalty.

🍬

Roshogolla

Soft, spongy cottage-cheese balls soaked in light sugar syrup.
